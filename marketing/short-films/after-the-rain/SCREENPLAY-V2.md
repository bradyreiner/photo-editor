# A Little Care

A 36-second, mostly wordless commercial for KIN, a fictional maker of small
utility robots. An adult park gardener and her wheeled helper. The emotional
payoff is a visible action: the flower she paints on him becomes the real flower
he brings her. Their friendship stays playful and present-tense.

## 0–6 seconds — The repair

A real camera's close view of the gardener's hands. She finishes tightening a
small plate on the robot's scuffed casing. A dent remains. She considers it,
then uses the paintbrush in her kit to turn the dent into the yellow center of
a crooked white daisy.

The robot leans its lens in too close to inspect her work. She gently pushes
his head back with one paint-stained finger. A tiny servo chirp; her quiet grin.
She taps the painted flower once: finished.

## 6–13 seconds — Partners

One continuous medium-wide shot in the park. She collects prunings; he holds
the bag open. He carefully squares the bag's rim. She drops in an unruly bundle
of branches, ruining the perfect rectangle. His lens looks at the bag, then at
her. She raises an eyebrow. He opens it wider.

The painted daisy is visible on his side, never announced in an insert.

## 13–21 seconds — The pause

The shower has passed. She sits on the bench with a cup, still reaching to
check the day's work list. The robot starts toward the next job, then notices
her rubbing a tired hand.

On the ground beside their collected prunings lies a single daisy with a bent
stem. His lens shifts from it to the flower on his own casing. A brief pause:
recognition, rather than a comic light bulb.

His gripper lifts the fallen stem with exaggerated care. It is still bent.
He makes one tiny adjustment, stops trying to perfect it, and turns to her.

## 21–30 seconds — The offer

The intimate two-shot developed in the painted keyframes. He holds the daisy
in the space between them. She initially offers her palm as if accepting a
tool. Then she sees what it is.

Her shoulders drop. She lets out a small, surprised laugh. The robot cocks
his sensor head, studying this very good result. Hold the shared look before
her fingers close around the stem. Let the acceptance land; do not cut early.

She tucks the daisy behind her ear. In the same shot, she brushes a speck of
dirt from the daisy painted on his side. Two small flowers, one returned gesture.

## 30–36 seconds — Together

A wider camera position as she stands. She carries the cup; he takes the
folded list. They head back toward their work, shoulder and lens briefly
turning toward each other. Their pace is easy.

The final brand line appears over the living shot:

**KIN. For each other.**

## Performance and camera

Natural adult anatomy. A practical machine with weight, wheels, joints, small
corrections and intentional pauses. Emotion comes from where the robot points
its lens and how long it waits. No face screen or human mouth on the machine.

Light and paint follow form: warm sun on cheek, fingers, enamel edges and
petals; cool reflected fill from the wet path. Detailed brushwork in the focal
exchange, broad painted edges elsewhere. A camera push must retain a single
shared scene; character animation must change the drawings themselves.

## Current production status

The improved painted hero frame and reaction keypose are developed. The native
brush motion test covers part of the offer, rather than the complete spot.
The opening repair, bag business, flower retrieval, handoff and closing walk
still require their own blocking and animated shots. The two flowers must be
included consistently in those final shots before the story can claim its payoff.

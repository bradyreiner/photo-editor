## Buyer read

The opening has a coherent visual identity. At 0.5 and 1.5 seconds, the oversized Looksy wordmark is crisp over the soft multicolor field. The lime `PHOTO + VIDEO / DESKTOP` kicker and bottom brand line support it without competing with the logo. The color field and the multicolor mark feel connected, and the dark center leaves the title area clear.

At 3 seconds, `One shoot. Every deliverable.` is a strong, immediately legible promise. The two-line supporting copy names camera RAW, finished photos, layered designs, and films. Large ivory type sits over a dark left-side treatment; even where the lower headline reaches toward the boats and lighter water, it remains readable. The boats give the statement a photographic subject instead of leaving the promise over a purely abstract card.

At 8 seconds, the `EDIT / DESIGN / CUT / DELIVER` chips add structure to the promise. They are distinct and readable at the delivered frame size. `CUT` is the only label that may require interpretation from a buyer who is not already a video editor; the subtitle says “films,” but the pill itself is insider shorthand. Consider `VIDEO` if the goal is immediate comprehension across photo and video buyers. This is a small copy question, not a layout failure.

At 11 seconds, the first product proof is a recognizable Looksy editing screen on the same boat photo. The image preview, Edit tab, Raw Exposure value, and White Balance controls make the photo-editing claim tangible. The inset is large enough in this 1920×1080 frame for the primary UI labels to read, while the headline remains separate above it. The exact frame evidence here supports the photo-editing introduction only.

## Priority notes

1. **Use the title-card hold to substantiate the cross-medium promise.** Frames at 3 and 5 seconds retain the same headline, subtitle, and boat image; at 8 seconds the category pills appear, and at 11 seconds the sampled frame shows photo editing. The frames do not establish whether there is enough motion between samples, so this is not a motion critique. From a buyer’s perspective, the opening names design, video, and delivery before these sampled frames show them. Keep the approved copy and length, but ensure the following beats show concrete, distinct proof for those claims (for example, a layered design, a real timeline edit, and an export result) rather than extending the same title image.

2. **Keep the initial photo editor proof.** It is the first point where the film moves from a promise about the product to a visible application state, and its screen hierarchy is legible. Any later proof should build from this rather than replace the opener with a generic interface montage.

3. **Optionally clarify `CUT`.** `VIDEO` is the clearest cross-audience category label. Do not change the other headline or subtitle based on these stills: they are readable, on-brand, and match the approved New Gods direction.

# LOOKSi launch platform / V2

16 November 2026 · working creative platform and production strategy

## What the first package missed

V1 delivered files before it developed a persuasive case. Five styles of typography did not amount to five campaigns. The audience was repeatedly described as people who edit photos and video; the copy rarely named the project they were trying to finish. The professional concept asked for scrutiny without giving someone a worthwhile project to scrutinize. The learner concept offered encouragement without teaching a visible decision. The joke could be read as a promise about file management. Too many cards said the tool was being built without showing why the work mattered.

V2 gives each campaign a distinct audience trigger, useful promise, proof path and response. The common subject is a tool Brady built for actual creative work. The voice changes because the situation changes: a candid introduction, a delivery problem, a first lesson, a recognizable editing habit and a statement of taste.

The campaign names are:

1. **I wanted to make this.** — Meet the builder and see what he is trying to make better.
2. **One project. Three finished pieces.** — Follow a photo, short video and carousel from one small project to delivery.
3. **Make this photo feel like the moment.** — Learn one visual decision, with enough explanation to try it yourself.
4. **The last tweak. Again.** — Recognize the moment confidence becomes another adjustment.
5. **Make room for your eye.** — Express the point of view that joins professional work and personal making.

The system should feel like one person speaking in five appropriate registers. Brady can be precise without sounding like a software specification and warm without making professional work sound trivial.

## Positioning

**Working positioning:** LOOKSi is the photo, video and board editor Brady is building around the way a visual project grows: choose a frame, shape its look, cut a sequence and make something ready to share.

**Short introduction:** “I’m Brady. I built LOOKSi, a photo and video editor with room for the boards and layouts that grow around the work.”

**Audience promise:** deliberate control for work someone needs to deliver, and a clear starting point for work someone wants to learn to make.

**Why this may matter:** the same person often owns the photograph, short cut, promotional graphic and final post. Each deliverable asks a different question, even when it comes from the same shoot. LOOKSi can be introduced through that actual project rather than a catalogue of features. Learning also becomes easier to show: explain the choice the creator wants to make, then reveal the tool that helps make it.

**Reasons to believe in the current product:** photo White Balance and Light controls; original comparison and Undo; a Timeline with trim/split and shared color controls; editable text and image boards; ordered board export surfaces. Current source inspection supports these as implemented surfaces and controller routes. The fresh campaign capture must demonstrate the selected route on the running build. The evidence ledger and exact source locations are in `campaigns.json`.

**Differentiation hypothesis:** breadth across photo, video and board work, with the maker’s decisions visible, will be a more credible opening than an unsupported claim of being faster, cheaper or more powerful than established editors. This is a proposition to test, not a conclusion that competitors lack these features.

Avoid a universal “replacement for everything” pitch. Avoid leading with local AI: it introduces runtime and privacy questions before the basic creative job is clear. A later campaign can demonstrate an actual local-agent workflow once that specific source-to-result run exists. These five campaigns do not need it.

## Audience segmentation by work

| Segment | Project trigger | Job to do | What they need to believe | Objection to resolve | Best first campaign |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Independent hybrid professional | A small brand, event or personal assignment needs several deliverables | Turn a consistent set of material into a finished image, short cut and social story | Each output is deliberately authored and actually exportable | “A broad tool will be shallow where I need precision.” | 02, then a deeper technical reply |
| Photographer learning short video | A stills project now includes a few clips | Make a coherent sequence without losing attention to the images | Timeline actions are understandable; existing photographic judgment still helps | “I’ll have to learn another entire craft before I can finish anything.” | 02 with the cut explained, then 03 |
| Enthusiast with a photograph they care about | A trip, evening, family moment or ordinary observation produced a photo that feels flat | Make the image feel closer to what they want to express | A small deliberate change can be understood and compared | “I don’t know what to move, or when to stop.” | 03, then 04 |
| Experienced maker who experiments | A personal edit has reached the “one more tweak” stage | Try a treatment, inspect it, and back out when it weakens the work | The product supports deliberate revision | “Another tool will get between me and a decision.” | 04, then 01/02 |
| Design-conscious creator and early follower | Finds a builder or sees a striking piece of work | Decide whether this project shares their taste and deserves attention | There is a person and a coherent creative direction behind the product | “It looks like another anonymous software launch.” | 05, then 01 |

These are situations, not demographic stereotypes. A paid photographer may be a beginner at video; an amateur may have excellent photographic taste. Say “if you’re learning this” rather than treating “hobbyist” as a low skill level. The creator’s project determines the explanation.

## The five campaign jobs

**01 / I wanted to make this.** A founder portrait with a visible product, not a life story that never reaches the work. The first ten seconds should establish Brady, LOOKSi and one actual creative action. The emotional tension is wanting capable tools without losing the thread of an idea. The response is a specific workflow request from a real maker. Never invent Brady’s employment history, years of frustration, customer count or nights spent building.

**02 / One project. Three finished pieces.** A small studio assignment: one subject, one image treatment, a three-shot sequence and a three-slide story. The tension is finishing several outputs while keeping a consistent point of view. Show manual decisions; do not disguise separate work as a one-click feature. The exported files and their relationship to the same source project are the proof. This campaign carries the strongest consideration burden and should not be published as a completed demo until those files exist.

**03 / Make this photo feel like the moment.** An actual lesson with one visible intention: a cooler camera rendering needs a warmer, quieter treatment, or a distracting edge needs a more deliberate crop. Select the intention after inspecting the chosen source. Do not prescribe a universal “right” setting. Show the original, explain the intention, make the adjustment, go too far briefly, pull back and compare. The reply should tell us what learners need explained next; saves matter because the post contains a usable idea.

**04 / The last tweak. Again.** A scene about taste and indecision. Start confident, move Temperature, reconsider, undo, recognize the loop. The founder is the subject of the joke. The product gets one credible appearance as the place the decision is made. A typographic file-name stack is insufficient. The response is another maker recognizing their own adjustment habit. The script must remain funny even if the viewer has never heard of LOOKSi.

**05 / Make room for your eye.** A short editorial film built from the rhythm of seeing and making: a light crossing a subject, a chosen frame, an adjustment, a cut, a board taking shape. Show the work more than the equipment. The phrase applies to paid assignments and personal work. Original optical RGB art supplies continuity, but it should occupy transitions and the signature rather than replace every scene.

## Source-to-result production design

Use an owned mini-project, ideally one with a real still photograph and original video clips of the same subject. If motion derives from a still or a generative source, describe it accurately as derived/source motion and use “project,” not “shoot.” A ceramic object near a window, a simple outdoor scene or a small studio setup can work. Choose based on available media and actual capture conditions, not on a promise to manufacture a perfect image. A recognizable person is unnecessary.

For 02, define three outputs before recording: a hero photo, a 10–15-second three-shot sequence and a three-slide 4:5 story. The photo gets an intentional color/light treatment. The sequence gets two visible edit decisions and a clear ending. The boards get one headline, one supporting detail and one closing image. Each output should look like the same project without claiming automatic synchronization between workspaces.

The source bank is shared; the stories are not. The 45-second professional hero is a narrative introduction. Separate 60-second photo and 68-second board proof scripts give controls, comparisons and files time to remain readable; a fast montage is not the only evidence. 01 uses an honest revision to explain the builder’s intent. 02 shows the complete delivery route. 03 slows down a single adjustment. 04 uses a short failed preference and Undo as the joke. 05 uses the strongest seconds as a portrait of creative attention. This produces production efficiency without making every post a reworded cut of the same film.

Keep one current-build screen master at readable desktop resolution and separate clean close crops for vertical films. Capture cursor-to-control-to-result continuity; only punch in after the viewer knows where they are. Keep the original media, build identity, uncut capture, final output and a short note of the actual actions. If capture is blocked, a dated real interface still can establish what the product looks like. It cannot substitute for an animation of a slider functioning or for a claim that an export succeeded. The preview variant in each campaign is written for that situation.

## Launch and funnel

The first wave is organic learning. It does not need a paid media budget before the message and release destination are ready.

| Stage | Audience question | Creative | Action | Evidence to record |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recognition | Who is making this, and why should I care? | 01 and 05 | Follow the build or describe a project | Qualified follows/profile visits and specific replies |
| Relevance | Does this help with something I actually do? | 03 and 04 | Save a useful idea or name an editing habit | Saves/shares plus context in replies |
| Consideration | Can it handle a concrete piece of work? | 02 and longer follow-ups | Request a workflow or inspect the output | Demo questions, output inspection and relevant requests |
| Access, when ready | Can I actually try or buy it? | A new version of the strongest proved demo | Visit a verified access destination | Destination clicks and attributed completed action |

Suggested sequence is relative to readiness: day 1 founder introduction; day 3 learner lesson; day 5 humor; day 8 complete project demonstration; day 11 editorial film; day 14 a direct answer to one real audience question. These are working slots, not publication commitments. If the 02 delivery capture is incomplete, move it rather than replacing the promised proof with a vague montage.

On Instagram, the caption should extend the image rather than transcribe every word. Carousels need a reason to swipe: a reveal, a decision or an ordered project. Reels start with action or tension and keep important UI readable. On Reddit, write an actual account of the project with builder disclosure, scope and a focused request; do not use the carousel captions as the body. The final community choice and current rules must come from the research/routing work, not from assuming a launch belongs in every photography subreddit.

Do not publish all five Reddit drafts as a burst. They are alternatives for different community contexts. Start with the one that genuinely fits. Return to answer replies with evidence and admit a limitation plainly when it is relevant. A follow-up demo answering a specific request is more useful than repeating the launch post.

## Experiment and measurement design

Each campaign has a distinct primary outcome because its job differs. 01 is useful workflow requests; 02 is qualified demonstration/output questions; 03 is saves and learning questions; 04 is shares with specific editing-habit replies; 05 is relevant profile visits/follows. Keep raw counts and denominator. Do not compare an Instagram save to a Reddit upvote as though they measure the same behavior.

For Instagram, calculate the requested action per 1,000 reached accounts when reach exists. If only impressions exist, use and label that denominator. For video, log duration, average watch time and the retention fields the account actually exposes. Record 24-hour, 72-hour and seven-day snapshots. A repeated viewer or Brady replying to a comment is not another independent expression of demand.

Test one creative variable at a time within a campaign: 01 face-first versus product-first opening; 02 finished-output-first versus project-brief-first; 03 intention-first versus before/after-first; 04 deadpan silence versus dry spoken line; 05 observational footage versus optical-light opening. Keep duration, body, CTA and source project fixed where possible. Publish timing, account growth and small organic samples limit inference, so describe observations rather than declaring statistical winners.

No invented benchmark, projected CPM or promised conversion rate is included. After two comparable observations, choose a next iteration based on both action rate and reply substance. A higher-view joke does not automatically replace the professional proposition. For any future paid test, approve the actual budget and verified destination separately; this package does not spend money or publish.

## Internal product boundaries

The existing brand palette is graphite, cool white and additive RGB. The current app still uses Looksy assets; LOOKSi is the campaign spelling requested here, with no source rename implied. Current commercial configuration targets Apple silicon Mac, has empty prices/checkout URLs and marks terms unapproved. Consumer copy therefore invites conversation or following the build, with no access/price claim.

Source-level implementation, dated native evidence, a current UI capture, and a fresh completed project are different evidence classes. Only the latter proves this particular source-to-result demo. The campaigns ledger maps each proposed shot and assertion to the evidence it needs. That separation should keep the public copy clear, not bury viewers under production notes.

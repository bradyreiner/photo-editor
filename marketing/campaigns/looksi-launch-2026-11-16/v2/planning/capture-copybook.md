Before moving a slider, try writing one sentence about the photo: what do you want it to hold onto?

I’m Brady, and I’m building LOOKSi, a photo and video editor. For the first photo walkthrough, I want the viewer to have a reason for the adjustment before learning the control.

The intention for this example is “warmer light, without losing the quiet.” It gives us something to judge. As Temperature moves, look at the whole picture. Does the warmth help the light feel the way you want, or has the color become the first thing you notice?

That is why the lesson includes going too far once. A strong trial can help you see the effect clearly. Pulling it back is a decision, not a failure to commit. The version you keep should help the photograph say what you meant.

Then look at the light. A small Exposure adjustment may support the treatment. It may also be unnecessary. If a change does not help your intention, you do not need to add it just because the control is there.

Finally, compare with the original at the same size and crop. Look away for a moment if you have been staring at it. Ask the sentence again: did the edit keep the feeling you wanted?

The numbers will depend on the image. This example is about learning what to look for, so there is no universal warmth or brightness setting to copy. Your photograph might need a cooler treatment, a different crop, or less editing altogether.

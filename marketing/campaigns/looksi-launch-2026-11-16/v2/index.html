I’m Brady. I built a photo/video editor, and I’d like to put it through a real project.

I’m Brady. I built LOOKSi, a desktop photo and video editor, and I wanted to introduce it through the work I care about making.

The idea is simple to say and fairly involved to build: start with a frame, shape its look, carry it into a sequence or a layout, and finish something you actually want to show someone. I want the little decisions along that path to feel considered.

That includes changing your mind. The adjustment you undo can tell you something useful about the image. The cut you move back two frames can be the one that makes the sequence feel right. I want to show those moments instead of presenting every edit as a clean march toward a predetermined result.

The current build has photo adjustments and comparison, a Timeline, and editable text/image boards. The attached builder letter includes a genuine archived tone-curve recording from 25 October and actual photo/board exports from 16 November. The companion casebook and recipe breakdown identify the source and output of each example.

I’m making this for people who need to deliver work and people who make it for themselves. A personal photo can matter a lot. A working photographer can be new to video. The project should determine how much explanation someone needs.

So I’d like to give the tool a specific brief from someone here. What do you make, and where does the edit get awkward? Maybe a set of event photos also needs a short clip. Maybe the picture has the right subject but the color never feels like the moment. Maybe the story needs a readable set of boards around it.

What would you put it through first? Tell me the media you start with and what a good finish would look like. I’m the builder, and I’ll use the replies to choose a real next demonstration.

Channel-native Reddit post draft

A small photo-editing breakdown: keep the warm peaks and the quiet blue layers.

I’m building LOOKSi, a photo and video editor, and wanted to share a small editing decision rather than a long feature list.

This Nepal photograph already had something I wanted to keep: warm light on the summit and cool, quiet layers below it. Before touching a control, I tried to name those two things. The aim was a slightly warmer, clearer reading of the same photograph.

For this particular JPEG, my combined treatment used White Balance → Temperature at 5900 K, Light → Contrast at +12%, and Exposure at +0.15 EV. Those numbers are choices for this image. A different source or a different intention may need something else.

The attached comparison uses actual PNG exports from the same native Looksy pipeline, with the same framing. It shows all three adjustments together; it is a recipe breakdown of the endpoint, rather than a recording of each individual control’s effect. The original source remains untouched.

My useful first step is to ask what the photograph already does well. Then make a modest choice, compare, and decide whether it helps. A personal photograph deserves that care just as much as a paid project.

What is one thing you would like to learn to change in your own photos? Or, looking at this image, what would you want to keep the way it was? I’m the builder and would like the next lesson to start with a real question.

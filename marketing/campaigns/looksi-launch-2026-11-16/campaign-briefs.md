# LOOKSi / five launch voices

Creative platform and production brief · 16 November 2026 · review package

The central message is the builder’s invitation: **“Look at this photo and video editor I’m building. I’m trying to make a better way for professionals and hobbyists to make their work.”** The word *better* is an ambition to demonstrate through specific choices and delivered work. It is not a claim of superiority across every workflow.

The campaign spelling **LOOKSi** follows the user’s current request. No product rename, release metadata change, or app identity change is included. The current app’s graphite, cool white and additive red/green/blue form the visual system. RGB is a light/material idea and an editorial accent, not a reason to fill every surface with saturated color.

## The marketing job

Earn qualified curiosity from people who edit photos, video, or both. Establish a recognizable maker’s point of view, then give each audience a concrete reason to ask for a demonstration. At this stage, a useful response is a real project, a precise workflow question, or a request to see the tool handle a relevant task. A broad impression count alone does not establish product demand.

The five voices are five ways into the same product, with separate jobs. The founder introduces the person and the intention. The professional demo earns scrutiny. The tutorial makes the first step approachable. Humor makes an editing habit recognizable. The editorial film expresses taste and creative authorship. Keep the underlying claim, source material and launch status consistent across them.

## Message hierarchy

1. **A person built this:** the founder speaks in the first person, explains the project clearly, and accepts scrutiny.
2. **It is for photo and video:** say the medium before listing specialist controls. Show both only when both are genuinely part of the recorded demonstration.
3. **People at different stages are welcome:** professional projects need deliberate control; personal projects deserve equal care. Avoid suggesting that beginners and professionals have identical needs.
4. **Creative decisions stay visible:** show a choice, a comparison, a revision and an actual result.
5. **Ask one useful question:** the CTA names a next conversation, not a vague demand for engagement.

Consumer-facing copy is in `copy.json`. Production caveats stay with the brief and tracker. A public post only needs a qualification when the audience could otherwise misunderstand the image, demonstration or availability.

## 01 / A note from the builder

**Role:** founder introduction. **Tone:** candid, thoughtful and unfinished in a useful way. The founder is a builder speaking directly to other makers. The post promises to show progress and asks what to test next.

**Visual direction:** a builder’s notebook translated into an editorial layout: graphite, cool white, simple underlines and small RGB registration marks. Actual desk footage should feel like the person’s real workspace. Show the face in the first beat of the full production edit; cut to a result quickly enough to make the medium clear.

**Deliverables:** a graphic announcement; an editable creative direction board; an 18-second motion review draft; a separate 24-second founder-led production master script. Optional four-panel social-carousel copy moves from the reason, to the audience, to the process, to a specific question; the rendered board is a creative direction sheet. Do not turn the founder’s experience into an invented customer testimonial.

**Recording:** allow 20 minutes. Capture three opening takes, one clean full read, five seconds looking at the work, and quiet room tone. Use an eye-level camera, a window or soft lamp, and a simple background. The main line is “I’m building a photo and video editor called Looksi.” The proof bank needs a real photo and a real short video from the same documented current-build session.

**CTA:** “What would make it better for you?” A qualified reply names a medium, project and difficult step. **Test:** change the opening between “I built this. I’d love your honest take.” and “What should I put this editor to the test on?” Keep the result, duration and CTA identical.

## 02 / Show the work

**Role:** proof-oriented professional consideration. **Tone:** restrained, exact and open to inspection. Say what is shown; let the result do the persuasion.

**Visual direction:** a quiet technical publication, with generous margins and thin indexed rules. Use legible close crops of real operations in the production edit. Never rebuild a decorative interface and present it as a functioning control. Let viewers look at the export for more than a single flash frame.

**Deliverables:** a typographic image, an editable direction board setting the evaluation standard, an 18-second motion review draft, and a separate 24-second screen-recorded master after capture. The full production version shows one photo, one short sequence, a real revision, and the exported files. Match the source, project and output throughout.

**Recording:** allow 60 minutes for a small project plus output inspection. Keep a complete uncut master, the build identity, source formats, final files and independent metadata. If an action takes longer than the edit shows, label the compressed segment. Do not display “real time” unless it is real time.

**CTA:** “Which step should I demonstrate next?” **Test:** exported result first versus editing gesture first. Keep the source, finished output and total running time fixed. **Success evidence:** a viewer asks a concrete professional question or requests a comparable workflow, rather than only complimenting the graphics.

## 03 / Start with your eye

**Role:** an approachable first lesson. **Tone:** patient and clear, with no implication that caring about photography requires expensive gear or a professional identity.

**Visual direction:** cool-white paper, readable graphite type and one small optical accent. Give every instructional verb its own beat. In the captured version, use a photograph with an obvious subject and explain the visual choice before naming the slider.

**Deliverables:** a welcoming “Your eye. Your first edit.” image, editable tutorial direction board, 18-second motion review draft, and a separate 24-second real-photo walkthrough script. “Choose what you want someone to notice → make one change → compare → choose” is an original teaching structure, not proof that every operation is equally simple.

**Recording:** allow 30 minutes. Record the original photo, one actual tonal adjustment, a same-source comparison and the exported result. Keep the desktop readable by punching into the relevant area. Do not stretch the entire desktop into a vertical frame. Video remains part of product scope; this specific lesson teaches a photo task.

**CTA:** “What part of editing feels confusing?” **Test:** question-led cover versus “Your eye. Your first edit.” **Success evidence:** saves and real learning questions. Do not conclude from saves alone that people used or understood the tool.

## 04 / The final final club

**Role:** recognition and conversation. **Tone:** dry, playful and affectionate toward the editing process. The joke is a familiar pile of “final” file names and the creative revisions behind them. It expresses the builder’s recognition of a habit. It does not assert that LOOKSi has automatic version management, fixes file organization, recovers lost work, or eliminates revisions.

**Visual direction:** oversized “final_final_actuallyfinal” typography, stacked fictional file-name cards and deliberate pauses. A simple text-only animation is a complete execution. These cards are graphic joke props, not a screenshot of a product file browser or a functioning version-history system.

**Deliverables:** playful image, editable direction board and 18-second original motion film. The four motion cards read “final_final.” → “final_final_v7.” → “I know the feeling. So I’m building.” → “LOOKSi. Photo + video. Tell me your file-name lore.” Product footage is unnecessary. Optional social-carousel copy and a separate 24-second “one more tweak” founder-skit script are included as future variants; they are not the current rendered film.

**Recording:** zero founder footage is required for the motion version. A future skit can add a pleased look, a doubtful look and a return to the screen. If any warmth/coolness adjustment is shown in-app, it must be real, from the same source and recorded session. Scripted lines are proposed performance, not customer testimony.

**CTA:** “Tell me your most chaotic file name.” **Test:** file-name setup versus cut-point setup, as separate future versions. Keep the joke’s duration and final question comparable. **Success evidence:** shares among relevant creators and specific replies explaining the revision behind the name; humor can reach people who never intend to edit, so inspect reply quality as well as reach.

## 05 / A point of view

**Role:** visual identity and memory. **Tone:** cinematic, editorial and personal. The promise is room for people’s decisions. The primary graphic and motion opening is “It starts with how you see.” Avoid an unsupported claim that Looksi uniquely creates originality.

**Visual direction:** original abstract optical motion: red, green and blue intersect into cool white on graphite. Use light, a deliberate cut and long typographic holds. The optical scene is authored campaign art. It is not an editing result, UI capture, or demonstration of product rendering.

**Deliverables:** editorial image, editable direction board and 18-second original motion film. A separate 24-second production script is an optional longer variant. An abstract film requires no founder shoot or product capture. Optional founder voiceover can make the “I’m building” line more personal. No cloned voice or synthetic founder likeness is part of the plan.

**Recording:** zero camera time for the original motion version; optional five-minute voiceover session. If replacing abstract imagery with an actual photo or film, retain correct source and author credits and show only work the maker is entitled to include.

**CTA:** “Follow the build.” **Test:** photograph/cut opening versus optical/light opening. Keep the same copy and duration. **Success evidence:** profile visits and replies describing actual upcoming work. This film expresses taste; it cannot qualify product performance.

## Shared production session

A single small owned shoot can supply photo and video to 01, 02 and 03. Pick one scene with a clear subject, still-image source and several short clips. Capture the photo edit, timeline edit, revision, final export and independent open of both files before making compressed social cuts. The uncut master remains the evidence source. It is acceptable to choose a modest project that works reliably; describe that project’s scope rather than generalizing it to every camera, codec or deadline.

Film founder material for 01 and optional 04 in the same 20–30 minute session. Record 02, 03 and 05 voiceovers together if wanted. Keep a clean voice track and use original or specifically licensed sound. A silent-readable version is required because all five ideas should remain understandable without sound.

Use one review cycle for the message and first frame, one for factual demonstration and delivered files, and one on a phone for legibility and crop. Keep substantive copy in the central safe area. Check the final placement preview because platform overlays vary. Export working sizes are 1080×1350 for feed images, 1920×1080 for creative direction boards and 1080×1920 for vertical video; these are production choices, not claims that each service has identical upload limits.

## Publication and measurement plan

Start with 01 as the introduction. Follow with 02 once complete recorded proof exists, 03 to establish usefulness for beginners, 04 for conversational variety, and 05 to consolidate the visual identity. The detailed relative-day sequence is in production-playbook.html. Sequence is relative to completed assets and review, not a promised launch date. Keep finished abstract campaign art distinct from the future founder or demo version so each can be reviewed independently.

Use a two-week working cadence with two or three distinct posts a week. Adapt each for its primary channel. For an individual community, choose the single most relevant question and disclose the builder connection. Verify the current community rules immediately before posting. Publishing and sending outreach are separate actions; this package does neither.

For each posted variant, record post ID, campaign ID, channel, format, hook, publication time, source/version, duration, reach or impressions, plays, available retention metric, shares, saves, substantive comments, profile visits and any verified destination clicks. Read once after about 24 hours, again after 72 hours, and at seven days. Treat those as observation windows, not promises that every platform exposes identical metrics.

Normalize against the denominator actually supplied. On Instagram, a per-1,000-reached rate is `count / reached_accounts × 1000`; if reach is absent, leave that measure blank and record a separately labeled impressions-based rate. For a Reddit text discussion, use substantive replies and the view metric actually exposed; do not compare a Reddit upvote directly to an Instagram save. Duplicate viewers, self-replies and duplicate cross-posts are not independent votes of demand.

There is no invented performance benchmark. Establish the account’s own comparable baseline, then test one variable at a time. A single post with small reach is directional evidence. Choose a next iteration when the audience signal and actual reply content agree; do not automatically declare the highest-view meme the product-positioning winner.

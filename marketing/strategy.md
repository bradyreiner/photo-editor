# Looksy marketing strategy

**Production status:** These are shoot briefs. Initial review cuts use labeled source imagery and development previews. See [capture status](evidence/current-capture-status.md) for the recorded evidence and remaining capture work.

## The identity

**Looksy. The creative future is yours.**

Looksy is a local creative studio for photographs, video, and visual design, with an agent the maker can direct. The emotional promise is creative independence: powerful tools should help more people bring their own ideas to life and honor the creator’s judgment. Say it with evidence on screen: request, result, review, revision, human choice, finished work.

The founder is the voice of the brand. The tone is candid, curious, opinionated, visually ambitious, and kind. A polished manifesto can sit beside a one-take “I was trying to fix this” clip. Imperfection is useful when it is honest; it is not a substitute for showing the product work. Treat trends as a changing set of story structures to try, not as a fixed brand voice.

**Positioning line:** A local creative studio for photos, video, and design—with an AI agent you direct.

**Recurring thought:** Your ideas. Your machine. Your direction.

**Voice rules:**

- Speak as the person building Looksy: “I built this because…”, “Here’s what I asked it to do”, “I changed my mind here.”
- Put the result or tension in the first seconds. Explain the feature after viewers have a reason to care.
- Show actual app footage and actual results from that run. Keep a correction, rejection, or manual finishing move in frame when it helps prove that the creator is in charge.
- Use precise capability language. Name Sony S-Log2/S-Gamut or S-Log3/S-Gamut3 when those are the actual sources and selected profiles. Say “a supported RAW file” only when the original camera file is visibly imported and decoded. Do not generalize one successful run into “any media” or “every camera.”
- A “local” claim must match the setup shown. For “this run stayed on my computer,” record the real local model and app state, verify the complete run with the network disconnected, and keep that proof with the capture. Otherwise say “I’m building a creative agent that runs locally” and show the actual model setting without making a broader privacy guarantee.
- Make the ambition human and concrete: more control, more room to experiment, more of a person’s own taste in the result. Avoid grand claims about replacing creative workers or tools.

## The campaign system


The two launch campaigns have different jobs:

| Campaign | Job | Proof to show | Core line |
|---|---|---|---|
| [The creative future is yours](campaigns/creative-control.md) | Make people care about the point of view and wonder what a local creative agent can do. | A real brief, an actual local-agent result, review, one creator-directed change, and a human-approved finish. | “The future of creative tools should leave you in the creative seat.” |
| [One shoot, many deliverables](campaigns/one-shoot-many-deliverables.md) | Make breadth tangible to photographers and creators who publish in more than one format. | One rights-cleared shoot feeding genuine RAW photo work, a real timeline edit from source footage, and an exported carousel. | “One shoot. Three ways to tell the story.” |

Aim the first wave at independent photographers, visual artists, and social creators who already make their own work and want more room to experiment. Keep the founder’s values visible, then let working creatives recognize their own needs in the demos. The best first CTA is conversation (“What would you make?” / “Which version feels more like you?”). Use “get early access” or “download Looksy” only when the destination is live and ready to serve that audience.

## A repeatable production loop

One deliberate capture session should create a small footage bank, not one overworked hero edit. Record a clean app run, founder camera lines, a clear view of the input assets, the result, and the final exported files. Keep an uncut master and record a brief evidence note before making fast versions.

1. **Pick one audience question.** Examples: “Does the local agent leave me in control?” or “Can I make my photos and video feel like one story?”
2. **Choose one real project and source set.** Prefer a shoot you own. For sourced media, check the exact asset license and human/brand context before downloading. Store the source page, creator, license text or saved terms, download date, attribution, releases, and a SHA-256 hash in a small rights ledger.
3. **Capture the whole proof path.** Show the starting material, actual controls, genuine agent or editing work, an intentional revision, and the exported result. Do not fake elapsed time, model output, file metadata, or offline state. If speeding up repetitive work, add “time compressed” on screen.
4. **Record three opening hooks for the same core demonstration.** Try a result-led opening, a personal confession/opinion, and a challenge/question. Do not also change the proof and the length in that hook test.
5. **Edit a native 9:16 master with readable captions.** Keep the face, app details, and captions clear of platform interface overlays. Reframe the same source into a square or landscape cut only when the footage benefits from it; do not stretch a desktop interface until it is unreadable.
6. **Create platform-native copy and audio.** Instagram/Facebook can use a quick visual payoff; Reddit needs the context and a real invitation for critique; LinkedIn gets the builder’s point of view and a legible workflow. For paid placements, use a track specifically cleared for ads. Keep any trend-audio experiment as a separate organic cut and confirm its current platform permissions before posting.
7. **Review on a phone, publish, and log the result.** Check that the hook reads silently, the exported work is visible, captions match spoken words, audio rights are clear, and every product claim is supported by the captured run.

## Trend scouting without chasing every sound

Once a week, spend 15 minutes looking at recent posts from photography, design, video, indie-software, and creator accounts. Save up to five references. Write down the mechanism rather than copying the post: confession, before/after, “I tried it so you don’t have to,” unusual constraint, fast build, reaction, comment reply, micro-story, or visual reveal. Note why a viewer might watch through, share, or answer.

Score each candidate from 0 to 2 on **Looksy fit**, **instant clarity**, **real product proof**, **rights/platform safety**, and **effort**. Reject any idea with a zero on rights or honest proof. Try ideas scoring at least 7/10. Keep the original premise and replace the borrowed details with your own footage, words, and result. If the trend depends on an audio track, check whether it is permitted for the specific post and intended use; a song available in a personal-use library is not automatically cleared for an advertisement. Avoid using audio as the only reason the post works.

Instagram Trial Reels can be used to expose eligible experiments to non-followers first; Meta says key metrics appear after roughly 24 hours and offers an optional automatic share decision based on performance over the first 72 hours. Treat that as a test surface, not a guarantee. [Meta: Trial Reels](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/12/trial-reels-try-content-non-followers-first-see-what-perfoms-best/)

For Instagram/Facebook paid Reels, Meta’s creative guidance emphasizes vertical 9:16 footage, audio, and keeping key messages in the safe zone. Use that as a production baseline, then check current placement preview before spending. [Meta for Business: Reels ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads)

Use Meta Sound Collection or another license that explicitly covers commercial ads for paid creative. Meta’s help page says its general Instagram licensed music library is intended for personal, non-commercial use and describes Sound Collection as available for commercial uses such as ads. Recheck the terms and account availability when choosing a track. [Meta: Instagram music library and Sound Collection](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/402084904469945)

For Reddit, lead with a compact TL;DR, then add context, media, and a focused discussion question. Reddit’s own Free-form Ad guidance recommends a TL;DR at the top, mixing media into longer copy, captions, and audience-specific explanations. Organic community posts should also follow each subreddit’s current rules and disclosure expectations. [Reddit Ads: Free-form best practices](https://business.reddithelp.com/s/article/Free-form-Ads)

## Sustainable two-week launch cadence

Publish three distinct creative ideas each week, then adapt their text and framing for the platforms where they fit. That yields six experiments in two weeks without pretending every cross-post is a new idea. The campaign briefs include alternate scripts to keep the next batch ready.

| Week | Main post 1 | Main post 2 | Main post 3 | Production focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Creative-control candid founder clip | One-shoot cinematic result reveal | Creative-control challenge with a real correction | Capture both projects; make three opening versions of one edit. |
| 2 | One-shoot RAW transformation | Creative-control comment reply or trend-form experiment | One-shoot carousel/timeline workflow | Reuse the first footage bank; capture pickups only where proof or clarity is missing. |

Cross-post only after writing a native post for each audience. Use Trial Reels for one new style if available. Leave a day between versions of the same core video on one account when practical, so they do not compete with each other. One weekly session can cover 60–90 minutes of app capture, 20 minutes of founder lines, and 2–3 hours of edit/copy/export. Keep a spare “small post” such as an answer to a real comment for weeks when a full capture is not possible.

## Experiment tracker and decisions

Maintain one row per post. Suggested fields:

`post_id | campaign | platform | publish_date | format/style | duration | hook_verbatim | first_frame | proof_shown | CTA | audio/license | source_asset_ids | hypothesis | reach/views | 3s views or hold metric | average watch time/completion | shares | saves | comments | profile visits | link clicks | follows/signups attributed | observation | next test`

Take a snapshot around 24 hours, 72 hours, and 7 days. Use each platform’s available retention measures; names and availability differ. Record raw results rather than treating a single viral post as a rule. Compare hook tests on the same platform, with similar duration and proof. Pick one winner for the next round only after the signal repeats. A useful post creates qualified curiosity: comments that show the viewer understands the workflow, relevant profile visits, saves/shares among creators, and signups or downloads when those are available. Views alone are not the goal.

Each post should answer one question. If a result underperforms, label whether the opening lost attention, the app detail was unreadable, the payoff came too late, the output was weak, or the audience was wrong. The next post changes one of those things. Do not infer that a feature is unpopular from one experimental edit.

## Rights-cleared demo media

Use your own capture as the default because it looks distinctive and provides real RAW/log evidence. Get a signed model/property release for recognizable people, private spaces, and commissioned work. Do not show real client metadata, private names, unreleased projects, or confidential files.

When stock is useful, download the asset from its source page, confirm the exact current terms and the specific file’s license, and preserve the page/terms in the rights ledger. Pexels says its photos and videos may be used commercially under the Pexels license; its restrictions still apply. Mixkit distinguishes a **Free License** from a **Restricted License**; only select a file marked for commercial use such as social posts and online marketing ads. [Pexels license](https://www.pexels.com/license/) · [Mixkit video license](https://mixkit.co/license/modal/videoFree/)

CC0 and public-domain assets can be useful for supplemental backgrounds, but verify the work really has that status and that no separate likeness, trademark, artwork, or property rights apply. CC0 addresses copyright and related rights held by the affirmer; it does not make every recognizable person or brand free to use in an endorsement-style ad. [Creative Commons CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)

Never use a trending creator’s post, music, screen capture, or distinctive creative as source footage without permission. Borrow the format’s broad storytelling mechanic, then make an original Looksy version.

## Capture-to-delivery checklist

- The source files, app state, agent response, and finished exports come from the same documented session.
- The actual agent/backend shown is local; any on-device/offline wording has a matching network-disconnected capture.
- A RAW claim shows the original supported RAW file. A log claim names the matched source/profile actually used. A color claim does not exceed the captured proof.
- No prompt or edit is staged as an agent result when the user performed it manually; narrate both accurately.
- Captions are readable on a phone and match speech. Social UI does not cover essential text or interface details.
- Every image, video, font, sound, and visible person has a recorded commercial-use basis and required permissions.
- Export variants are reviewed at their actual target aspect ratio; the result remains legible and photographic.
- The post’s CTA destination is live, and its final caption accurately describes the demonstrated behavior.

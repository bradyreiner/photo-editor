# Scene grain and halation

Scene's Film inspector now offers **Creative film** and **Film plane · physical
sizes**. Existing projects keep the creative model and its original image output.
The film-plane model is an explicit, fast statistical approximation. It does not
claim to reproduce a commercial film, scanner, camera response, individual silver
crystals or full spectral layer transport.

## Controls and behavior

- Grain strength, correlation length in microns, and a new grain pattern action.
  A saved seed fixes the pattern; resolution changes do not choose another seed.
- Halation strength, diffusion sigma in microns, and separate RGB return
  fractions. Returning light retains the incident RGB instead of converting all
  emitters to a scalar red halo. The physical-size mode has no luminance cutoff.
- A recording format for cameras without physical calibration. It is centre
  cropped to image aspect. A physical camera inherits its actual frozen active
  gate, including separately rounded horizontal and vertical pixel pitches.
- Film state persists in saved views and camera paths. Edits to tone or camera
  post effects preserve the film model. Zero effect amounts preserve the neutral
  image exactly. The original project is unchanged.

Open `build/scene-deliverables/bonsai-film-plane-2026-10-26/project` in Scene and
recall a saved view. The adjacent four PNGs show neutral, grain, halation and
combined processing from the same configured lens/lighting/pose. The combined
recipe deliberately uses prominent grain (strength 0.6, correlation 20 microns),
halation 0.4, diffusion sigma 90 microns and RGB return [0.8, 0.25, 0.04].

## Model and limits

`gaussian_film_plane.h` constructs a continuous stationary density field using
128 deterministic Fourier modes. Frequencies approximate the Gaussian spectrum
whose spatial covariance is exp(-distance² / (2 correlation²)). Each mode is
analytically averaged over the actual rectangular pixel with two sinc factors.
This reduces unresolved grain variance without changing its realization or
introducing Monte Carlo integration noise. It is a finite spectral approximation,
not an implementation of the IPOL discrete grain algorithm.

Grain perturbs logarithmic density before the display shoulder/contrast. Its
generic exposure-dependent amplitude uses occupancy H/(1+H), and the Gaussian
log-normal mean compensation uses the filtered field variance. The amplitude
curve is not fitted to measured sensitometry. In particular, ISO, absolute
photons, wavelength-dependent dye clouds and scanner MTF are not recovered from
display-referred splat colors. The resolved photograph is generated by the
existing thin-lens renderer; validated compound glass rays are not yet connected.

Halation uses normalized, separable RGB Gaussian diffusion in linear exposed
light, truncated at three sigma, with zero extension beyond the photograph.
RGB return fractions parameterize available returning light. This is a diffusion
approximation to film-plane light return, not a layered radiative-transfer or
backing-reflection ray simulation. It adds no light to black and does not create
a color channel absent from its source. Post processing and tone mapping apply
after this light contribution.

The physical scale and pixel integration are motivated by
[continuous-resolution grain research](https://www.ipol.im/pub/art/2017/192/).
The [Image Permanence Institute's backing-layer explanation](https://www.filmcare.org/vd_antihalation.php)
describes why returned light causes halation. Those sources do not validate this
particular approximation or its default parameters. No GPL implementation was
imported.

  30.8-33.1 seconds per variant with six tiles. This path rerendered the optical

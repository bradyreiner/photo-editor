# Carousel Delivery 100 Checklist

This checklist turns the current Carousel workspace into a deliverable social-design product rather than a capable but unfinished canvas. It is grounded in the July 19, 2026 workspace screenshot and the current implementation in `CarouselView.qml`, `carousel_document_controller.*`, and `carousel_document_controller_test.cpp`. It is explicitly functionality-first: authoring depth, saved-state truth, output control, speed, and recovery outrank onboarding or instructional UI.

The existing foundation is real and should be preserved: multi-board documents, four output ratios, slot and freeform media, editable text/shapes/brush strokes, grouping, alignment, guides, undo/redo, saved board templates, basic brand kits, save/reopen, photo Edit round trips, PNG/video export, and focused controller tests. The items below are additions or upgrades beyond that baseline.

## Product goal

Make Carousel a fast, precise production workspace for turning edited photo and video sets into multi-board deliverables. Users should be able to build, batch-edit, version, validate, and export complex carousels directly; the product should expose useful operations, not walk people through a prescribed process.

## Completion rules

- Keep every item unchecked until its visible behavior and applicable undo, save/reopen, export, accessibility, and performance proof pass.
- Do not count a button, placeholder, or static mock as completion. The result must affect the saved document and exported output truthfully.
- Do not add onboarding tours, coach marks, goal questionnaires, forced wizards, or tutorial overlays. Prefer direct commands, good defaults, discoverable shortcuts, and visible state.
- Preserve the repo's no-ellipsis UI contract: labels may resize or wrap, but important visible text must not be silently truncated.
- Keep the default workspace calm. Advanced controls belong in contextual sections, focused dialogs, or menus, not as more permanent rows in the inspector.
- **P0** blocks core creation, persistence, performance, or delivery, **P1** materially improves daily usefulness, and **P2** adds differentiation after the core path is dependable.
- Every template must use ordinary editable layers, include purposeful sample copy and media guidance, adapt to supported ratios, and survive save/reopen and export.

## Delivery order

1. **NOW — functional release:** finish every P0 item, prove direct creation and batch editing on a five-to-twenty-board document, and close every persistence, performance, and export gate.
2. **NEXT — daily production depth:** finish P1 once the P0 workflow is stable; expand templates, reusable systems, cross-board control, and format variants.
3. **LATER — differentiation and polish:** finish P2 without increasing default-workspace clutter or weakening document/export truth.

# Video insertion, adjustment deletion, and brush responsiveness

This investigation exercises the complete Looksy QML shell on native macOS Cocoa/Metal, using isolated preferences, generated media, and recovery persistence. It targets the workflows reported on October 5 rather than treating every prior audit finding as a new defect. The broader inventory remains in [the performance audit](performance-impact-audit-2026-09-28.md).

## Findings and retained changes

| Workflow | Capability already present | Actual gap and change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add video to Timeline | Background media probing, poster extraction, waveform generation, and warm caches | Applying warm caches published incomplete intermediate clip state and rebuilt QML projections repeatedly. Append, insert, overwrite, and Source append now batch this synchronous work and publish the completed selection once. Cold worker completions still publish their results. |
| Delete adjustment layers after painting | Immutable history capture and a background formatter for large binary payloads | Hidden History bindings eagerly requested detailed diffs on every revision. The hidden panel now releases its history model. Deletion callback median fell from 27.99 to 6.37 ms; its history stage fell from 9–36 ms to 0.21–0.37 ms. |
| Paint while History is visible | Dedicated paint raster worker and history formatting worker | Compressed paint tiles are strings and did not qualify for the existing binary-payload threshold. History now recognizes paint tiles and formats their diffs on the worker, including small compressed tiles. The focused controller contract verifies deferred formatting and correct labels. |

The deletion measurements above are sequential diagnostic runs, not three alternating pairs. They establish the synchronous history cost; they do not establish an improvement in final painted-frame latency. In those runs, median full-shell paint settle time varied from 284 to 402 ms despite the much cheaper history stage. [Baseline](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/initial-native-baseline/responsiveness.json), [hidden History change](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/hidden-history-after/responsiveness.json), [batched insertion](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/batched-insertion-after/responsiveness.json).

### Accepted insertion notification scope

A phase trace found that video insertion also notified unchanged document identity and Media organization properties. `timelineStateChanged` consumed about 18 ms and `smartCollectionsChanged` about 8 ms in measured warm adds. The four insertion methods now publish editorial state without notifying unchanged timeline identity, saved paths, bins, smart collections, review ranges, metadata organization, and Source tape. Other operations retain full publication. Clip, selection, sequence, playback, markers, multicam, Source monitor, and media-analysis notifications remain.

Three alternating before/after pairs use the same driver objects, engine libraries, and frozen source QML. An isolated reference executable differs only in the `emitAllState` body, which ignores the new editorial-only flag and emits the original signals. Both executables contain the accepted cache batching and History changes. This isolates the additional notification-scope improvement from prior changes.

## Native comparison protocol

The new `--responsiveness-proof-dir` mode in `actual_app_workflow_benchmark_driver` measures six pixel strokes, six adjustment deletions, and six video appends. It records synchronous call duration, the next UI frame, a subsequent settled frame, and the largest 5 ms heartbeat gap. The next UI frame is **not** a guarantee that changed brush pixels or a decoded video frame were presented.

Each run checks that deletion preserves the exact underlying paint recipe, undo restores the adjustment, redo restores the exact paint recipe, a flattened export decodes successfully, and a recovery journal remains nonempty. Export parity compares decoded 16-bit RGBA pixels rather than PNG compression bytes. Fixture catalog helpers deliberately fail, leaving the recovery journal authoritative for the fixture; the visible save-warning banner is expected. Successful database saves and crash/relaunch recovery are separate qualifications.

The native acceptance target is at least 20% reduction in median callback time, with no more than 20% regression in median frame, settle, heartbeat, or maximum-RSS measurements. A 2 ms absolute timing tolerance excludes submillisecond timer noise. Repeated runs aggregate medians of run medians. Native Cocoa/Metal, exact exported pixels, exact recipe undo/redo, nonempty recovery journals, and RSS availability are mandatory guards. Source and binary provenance is retained with the comparison.

Two early reference runs were invalid: direct writes to `currentWorkspace` raced the shell's pending navigation and returned to Edit. They produced no insertion measurements and are excluded. The fixture now uses `switchToWorkspaceIndex`, matching the application's navigation flow.

## Remaining measured work

- Paint raster work is already asynchronous. The persistence-disabled native pixel-paint fixture still measured approximately 67–98 ms to its first painted presentation at 2400 pixels wide; render work was about 31–37 ms. Removing history formatting does not eliminate rendering, image publication, or GPU/CPU transfer cost. [Raw native paint baseline](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/minimal-paint-native-baseline.json).
- With persistence enabled, native traces show recipe preparation and helper startup on the UI thread taking roughly 30–50 ms as painted payloads grow. Immediate accepted-edit recovery checkpoints also remain synchronous. A later optimization must preserve recovery authority and durability, rather than delaying an accepted edit's checkpoint.
- Whole-track QML projection and selection/playback bindings still cost time after insertion. This fixture contains six repeated video adds with warm file metadata; large mixed-media sequences, slow storage, concurrent imports/exports, 4K decoding, and sustained playback require separate workloads.
- Ordinary frame clocks in the insertion fixture do not prove decoded-video smoothness. Optional `--responsiveness-decoded-video` runs the existing decoder-backed continuous-scrub probe and records readiness and processed-frame metrics separately. Empty metrics or failed readiness are not accepted playback evidence.

## Runtime correctness and limits

The visible-History native run retained detailed rows after six strokes and six deletions, restored the adjustment with undo, restored the exact paint recipe with redo, and exported the same decoded pixel hash as the paired insertion runs. [Report](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/visible-history-decoded/responsiveness.json), [visible History](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/visible-history-decoded/photo-after-brush-and-delete.png).

The separate decoder-backed scrub check had `mediaReady=true`, both warmup readiness checks passed, and 48 requested updates produced 48 processed frames. Latest-input-to-frame p95 was 33 ms and maximum 60 ms at a 25 Hz input cadence. Processing ran at 293 × 195 monitor resolution on the generated H.264 fixture. This verifies that decoded frames participated in this small workload; it does not qualify 4K footage, color/effect-heavy playback, or sustained 60 fps.

The final real-photo mask-brush workflow had `target_events_met=true` and the painted-Canvas/sync/frame-swap receipt contract. First-use feedback was 12 ms, but warm feedback p95 was 76.55 ms against the 50 ms gate. Point dispatch was 0 ms; the delay was before Canvas painting/synchronization (65–74 ms). This is a valid failing smoothness result. [Report](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/real-photo-mask-brush-gate.json).

A further Canvas scheduling candidate removed the zero-interval timer turn and requested painting directly. Frozen QML before/after runs on the same real photo did not meet the target: median run p95 changed from 121.7 to 120.6 ms, less than 1%. The candidate was never applied to product source. Two valid pairs are retained; the first pair is excluded because its reference run overlapped focused tests. All retained workflow runs had their target events, but failed the brush smoothness budget. [Decision and raw reports](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/brush-canvas-candidate/decision.json). The remaining delay therefore needs render/publication work rather than that timer adjustment. This investigation stops the bounded loop with those limits explicit.

## Additional interaction audit

The follow-up investigation initially found four additional interaction hotspots. The read-only findings below motivated the measured fixes in the following section.

1. **Long-Timeline UI projection.** `timelineTracks()` reconstructs maps for the complete sequence before QML filters the visible clips. The existing row/clip virtualization therefore does not bound this controller work. Multi-selection operations also emit `clipsChanged` and `sequenceChanged`, even when clip content has not changed. A freshly rebuilt controller benchmark measured projection p95 of 1.7–2.4 ms at 100 clips, 15.4 ms at 1,000 clips, and 75.0–75.4 ms at 5,000 clips across eight tracks. Undo capture at 5,000 clips was only 0.67–0.72 ms, and public trim was 4.3–4.7 ms. This moves projection ahead of undo copying as the measured priority in this workload. These are headless controller timings, not native frame timings. [Raw measurements and provenance](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/additional-audit/provenance.json); sources: `TimelineDocumentController::timelineTracks`, `selectAllClips`, `selectClipsForward`, and `TimelineView.sequenceTrackModel`.
2. **Leaving Edit or changing photos processes the whole recipe again.** `Main.syncEditorRecipeToCatalog` requests the complete asset-bound patch and calls `CatalogAssetModel::applyAdjustmentPatchJsonToAssetIds`. The catalog parses it, parses its existing record, merges groups, and serializes the result before checking whether anything changed. Large paint/mask payloads can therefore add synchronous work to an otherwise simple navigation action. Index lookup is already efficient and persistence is queued; the remaining candidate is this repeated recipe processing, not blocking database execution. Sources: `Main.qml` around line 1496 and `catalog_asset_model.cpp` around lines 4265–4382.
3. **History hover starts full preview renders.** Every eligible row's hover enter/leave calls `setEditHistoryPreviewIndex`. Resident revisions apply immediately; entering and leaving submit full-pipeline preview intents. Disk restoration already uses a worker and generation cancellation, but there is no hover dwell/debounce at the QML call site. Moving across many history rows can generate repeated render requests while the user is simply navigating the panel. Sources: `StudioWorkspace.qml` around line 17369, `editor_preview_controller.cpp` around lines 18708 and 27020.
4. **Layer-based Photo Fragment tips are prepared before stroke feedback.** With `textureMaterial=photo_fragment` and `tipLayerId`, `beginPhotoPaintStroke` rasterizes the tip layer at its full dimensions and scans/crops its alpha synchronously, before admitting the paint job. This differs from saved tip presets, which already have a decoded-image cache. A large layer used as a tip can therefore delay stroke start despite the raster worker used later for painting. Sources: `editor_preview_controller.cpp` around line 29662 and `PhotoFragmentBrushPresetStore::cropToVisiblePixels` around line 165.

The audit itself changed only benchmark instrumentation. After approval to fix these findings with Luna agents, three agents implemented recipe handoff, History hover, and layer-tip reuse; the coordinating agent implemented Timeline projection and reviewed and measured all four changes.

## Accepted fixes from the additional audit

| Target | Reference | Retained change | Evidence |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 5,000-clip Timeline projection, median of run p95 | 88.81 ms | 16.88 ms, 81.0% faster | [Comparison](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/luna-fixes/timeline/comparison.json) |
| Unchanged 5.6 MB recipe handoff, median of run means | 24.47 ms | 5.81 ms, 76.3% faster | [Comparison](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/luna-fixes/recipe/comparison.json) |
| Preview requests during 12 rapid History hover enter/leave pairs | 24 | 0 | [Native pointer-input comparison](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/luna-fixes/hover/comparison.json) |
| Warm layer-based Fragment stroke admission, median of run medians | 16.38 ms | 0.058 ms, 99.6% faster | [Native comparison](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/luna-fixes/fragment/comparison.json) |

**Timeline projection:** Inactive and unselected clips now produce the exact compact UI record directly, avoiding construction and filtering of a full inspector record for every clip. Active and selected clips retain their full inspector records. This introduces no projection cache. The contract compares all 60 compact fields against the prior full-record filtering across audio, video, captions, adjustment clips, proxy/offline state, retiming, groups, and multicam. Exact history restoration also passes. Three alternating pairs used 5,000 clips across ten tracks. Capture, trim, selection, and RSS guards passed. These are controller CPU timings, not a 5,000-clip native frame-rate qualification. Selection publication and whole-sequence enumeration remain.

**Recipe handoff:** A single existing photo asset can skip repeated parsing and normalization when its incoming bytes match the stored bytes and the existing projection cache confirms that those exact bytes are canonical. The guard excludes group overrides and transient promotion. A cache miss, legacy inner fields, pretty-printed input, clipboard edits, and changed input retain the original path. SHA1 identifies a cache entry; an exact string comparison validates its contents. There is no new cache. The focused test executes the actual extracted `Main.syncEditorRecipeToCatalog` body and verifies normalization, group override, clipboard profile binding, and changed recipe behavior. Three alternating pairs used a real 2 MiB segmentation mask serialized to a 5,609,322-byte canonical recipe. The changed workload was 99.64 versus 102.60 ms and passed the guard; RSS also passed. This measures the catalog apply call on a warm canonical cache, not the entire navigation action or initial editor serialization.

**History hover:** A 120 ms dwell starts a preview only while the pointer remains on an eligible revision. Leaving the row cancels or clears it; changing tabs, hiding the inspector, switching workspace/page/asset, editing history, or clicking Restore cancels pending work. Native QPA pointer input in the complete Cocoa/Metal shell exercised rapid traversal, a short hover, sustained preview, immediate leave, hidden-panel cancellation, and Restore before the timer fires. The reference left an active preview behind when hiding History; the retained change clears it. Both runs restored exact recipe/history state and exported identical decoded pixels. Native captures were visually reviewed. This is a deterministic request-elimination and cancellation result from one matched native pair, not a general rendering-latency benchmark. [Preview capture](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/luna-fixes/hover/after/history-hover-preview.png).

**Photo Fragment layer tips:** A one-entry cache reuses the exact prepared layer image when asset identity, layer identity, dimensions, pixel data, paint tiles, and draft tiles match. The original rasterization and alpha crop run on a miss. Retention is conservatively bounded to 64 MiB including source keys and image storage; oversized or invalid tips clear the cache. Changing assets clears it, and source edits or undo cause an exact-key miss. The contract verifies image and color-space equality, reuse, source mutation, restoration, and asset separation. Three alternating native pairs used a 2,048-pixel tip source on an 1,800 × 1,200 canvas. All six decoded RGBA64 exports matched SHA256 `19a57302be5c94673c5fbf5c6e8cd8b4bd4c6ff9e76979caacf6b0f44ad3de9a`. First painted presentation improved from 159.07 to 128.15 ms; completed-stroke presentation improved from 167.38 to 135.26 ms. Cold admission remained approximately 27 ms. Frame, release, input, commit, export, and RSS guards passed. An earlier 1,200-pixel-wide fixture placed its final stroke outside the canvas and is retained as invalid evidence, excluded from comparison. Cold preparation remains synchronous and full paint presentation still exceeds a 60 fps budget.

The Timeline, recipe, and Fragment comparisons each use three alternating pairs, at least 20% target improvement, timing guards of 20% with a 2 ms noise allowance, and a 20% RSS guard. No timing run overlapped compilation. Reference binaries share fixture objects and other runtime objects with the paired after build, replacing only the targeted implementation. After recipe timing, one additional normalization regression assertion was added and passed; its benchmark fixture and timing loop were unchanged. The History pair shares the final driver and frozen QML except for the hover change. [Final source/binary provenance](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/luna-fixes/provenance.json).

The final desktop build passed. Five focused tests passed together in 9.80 seconds: Timeline history/projection, recipe handoff, Fragment cache, insertion publication, and history storage. [Build log](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/luna-fixes/final-checks/build.log), [test log](evidence/interactive-stalls-2026-10-05/luna-fixes/final-checks/tests.log). Both native hover runs also processed all 48 requested decoded scrub frames with media readiness, p95 input-to-frame of 39 ms, and exact paint/delete/undo/redo/export parity. ## Zoom-out image coverage bug

After a zoomed edit, a source-size native texture can contain only its visible rendered patch. Two defects allowed that small box to remain after returning to Fit: the coverage predicate used `QRegion::contains(QRect)`, which tests overlap rather than complete coverage, and clearing the viewport did not request missing full-image pixels. [Qt's rectangle-overlap semantics](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qregion.html#contains-1).

The controller now requires the entire requested region to be covered, including overlapping pans and zoom-out expansion. Leaving an active viewport with incomplete coverage queues a complete preview for the existing edit generation, using the current recipe and normal caches. It does not create an edit, history entry, or persistence event. Restoration excludes the old brush dirty rectangle and visible-only tile restriction. It waits behind an in-flight render, and skips duplicate work if that render already filled the whole image. Complete resident pixels still serve ordinary zoom/pan without another render.

The new `editor_preview_zoom_out_coverage_test` reproduces partial backing after an exposure edit, checks both a finished patch and a delayed regional result, verifies exact recipe/history/generation preservation, and checks resident reuse. The existing pixel-zoom fixture also gained a partially overlapping viewport assertion. The new coverage test failed before the change with `zooming out must restore all edited pixels without another edit`; coverage and resident-reuse tests pass after it.

Native Cocoa/Metal proof zooms to 4×, performs two real brush edits and adjustment-delete/undo/redo checks, then deterministically recreates a missing resident base through the production regional publication path. The patch pixels come from the actual edited Metal texture. Returning the real QML viewport to Fit reproduced the small-box symptom in the reference build. The changed build restores full coverage and exactly matches the complete edited native texture, SHA256 `0329a9dee0d7f8a064c4cce7880b44d3c6067444bb72c5afbb133030fbcd10c2`, without another edit. Both runs retain exact recipe/history state and identical exported pixels, SHA256 `f76182b5984f9d77cd664c0a08e718ca87de2bad159076dcf771ad6942b52708`. [Reference capture](evidence/zoom-out-coverage-2026-10-05/before/zoom-out-fit.png), [fixed capture](evidence/zoom-out-coverage-2026-10-05/after-final/zoom-out-fit.png), [native report](evidence/zoom-out-coverage-2026-10-05/after-final/zoom-out.json), [comparison and provenance](evidence/zoom-out-coverage-2026-10-05/comparison.json). Captures were visually reviewed; the save-warning banner is expected from the isolated failing catalog helper.

The fixture uses generated 1,800 × 1,200 media, frozen source QML, temporary settings/catalog data, and `QT_SCALE_FACTOR=0.5` so Fit exits source-pixel inspection. Regional base loss is injected to isolate the publication state; this is not a claim that every zoomed edit naturally loses its resident base. An initial default-scale run stayed in pixel inspection at Fit and failed the fixture's inactive-viewport condition. Two early after checks sampled a finished worker before its result was published; their premature native readbacks are excluded. The final fixture waits for publication, complete current coverage, and processing idle before comparing pixels. Its photo-specific path avoids unrelated Timeline measurements. No timing improvement or frame-rate qualification is claimed here.

The desktop application and native proof driver builds passed. Zoom-out coverage and resident reuse tests pass. The broader existing pixel-zoom test also reaches an outline-simplification assertion failure outside the coverage path; its before/after status is retained in [validation](evidence/zoom-out-coverage-2026-10-05/validation.json). The full suite is not claimed to pass.

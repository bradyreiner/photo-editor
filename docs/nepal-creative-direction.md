# Nepal: editorial direction reset

2026-11-15. Research and visual studies following the user's rejection of the
Unfolded composition. These initial studies informed the subsequent
[Near & Far production pass](nepal-near-far-production.md): six saved native
boards, nine original photographs and 41 editable layers. The studies below
remain exploratory references. No saved Looksy document was changed during the
initial research pass; the production pass created its own separate document.

## What the work needs to achieve

Working brief: make a viewer feel present in the trip through movement, human
connection, walking, changing light and the eventual mountain reveal. The user's
original request calls for an adventurous photographic and graphic-design feel.
The precise emotional priority is still open; a question was sent to distinguish
personal presence, mountain awe and expedition energy. In the meantime, the
personal photographic-story interpretation is the working assumption.

The previous design had devices but little editorial intent. Its huge country
name consumed photographic space without adding meaning. The cutout made the
portrait function like a poster effect. A lime sequence curve connected places
graphically without conveying useful information or a personal observation.
The continuous montage exposed everything at once, suppressing discovery and
contrast between dense and quiet moments. Successfully retaining editable layers
and exporting the document did not establish artistic success.

## Three studies

Local originals, SVG sources and rendered previews are in
`.photo-editor/nepal-inspiration/`. Photos are cropped within authored vector
layouts; production photographs were not regenerated.

1. **The days between:** personal documentary emphasis. A shared night view and
   a small tactile encounter support a large candid expression. A cobalt interval
   separates the title from the photographs. This has the clearest human premise;
   its current rectangular arrangement still needs more fluid sequencing.
2. **Warmth, carried:** visual connections through existing colour. Flowers,
   firelight and dawn light provide the relationship. This is useful as a
   sequencing rule; the current inset treatment is still too arranged to be a
   complete direction by itself.
3. **A sense of scale:** an almost uninterrupted mountain scene with a much
   smaller human photograph. Useful for a quiet late spread. As an entire design
   system it would be too restrained and too close to a conventional travel cover.

The recommendation combines the human premise of the first, selective visual
connections from the second and a quiet mountain reveal from the third. The
working titles are editorial proposals, not quotations from the user.

## Proposed sequence

1. **Enter through motion:** a street photograph with actual camera movement.
   Establish immediacy before adding explanatory copy.
2. **Meet the people:** a shared view and a candid expression. Give gestures and
   faces priority over typography.
3. **Feel the walk:** a path, a walker, nearby detail. Alternate photographic
   distance; do not repeat scenic hero images at identical scale.
4. **Notice a connection:** select one colour or shape already present in two
   photographs. A flower/firelight or cable/path match should be earned by the
   images, without an arbitrary drawn connector.
5. **Open out:** let the mountain photograph occupy the spread without a headline
   covering its peak. This is the release after the denser scenes.
6. **Return to a person:** finish with a human moment so the story stays personal.

This is an associative photographic essay, not an asserted chronological
itinerary. Actual memories or short notes from the user would support more
specific captions. Until then, avoid simulated handwriting, invented ticket
stubs, fake routes, borrowed cultural decoration and fabricated personal quotes.

## Acceptance questions for the next board

- Does each photograph advance a distinct observation or emotional beat?
- Does the sequence create anticipation, contrast and a clear final impression?
- Can the photographs carry the premise before the headline is read?
- Are subjects and important gestures preserved by the crops?
- Does each graphic element have a legible job?
- Does each exported board work alone as well as in sequence?
- Are the original photographs and final native layers independently editable?

These are review questions, not invented numerical aesthetic scores. Further
production work should follow the chosen premise and a visual review of the
studies; avoid another proliferation of saved draft documents.
